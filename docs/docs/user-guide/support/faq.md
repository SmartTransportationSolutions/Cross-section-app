# Frequently asked questions

## Can I use STS Street for professional presentations or publications?

Yes, absolutely! Use STS Street however you like, but [please provide attribution](#attribution).

## What is the end user license?

STS Street is based on Streetmix and keeps its two licenses: the **software** uses the [GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 or later](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html) (AGPL), while the **content** (street illustrations and derived graphics) uses a [Creative Commons CC-BY-SA 4.0 license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). One or both of these licenses might apply to your situation.

The **AGPL license** is an open-source software license. This means you may copy, modify, and reuse the code base, as long as the original copyright notices remain (Code for America and contributors, Streetmix LLC, and Smart Transportation Solutions for the STS Street modifications) and you make your modified source code available if you host a publicly accessible service. The complete source code of the version you are using is always available from the **Source code** link in the editor footer.

The **Creative Commons CC-BY-SA license** governs the illustrations, as well as any derived graphics. You may modify and republish anything you make in STS Street as long as you [provide attribution](#attribution). You must also allow others to reuse or republish your STS Street-created content.

This is a basic overview of what these licenses mean. For more details, including how these licenses may apply to your specific use case, please refer to the license text or consult with a qualified legal advisor.

## How do I provide attribution? {#attribution}

When you reuse or republish content created in STS Street, you should include the following messaging:

- That you created this content with STS Street, which is based on Streetmix
- That the content is licensed under Creative Commons CC BY-SA 4.0 (or the AGPL license, if the reproduction includes code)
- Include a link to STS Street and to Streetmix ([https://streetmix.net/](https://streetmix.net/))

We are flexible with attribution. You may be in a situation where you're not able to include all or some of those things. That's okay! But you may not _choose_ not to provide attribution. Please use your best judgment about what form of attribution is viable for you.

## Can I copy the STS Street code or set up my own instance?

You sure can. The complete corresponding source for the deployed version is offered at the **Source code** link in the editor footer, and the repository README and `docs/operations.md` describe installation with Docker Compose.

## Does STS Street support Internet Explorer? {#internet-explorer}

Our unique interface means STS Street is designed with the most recent web technology in mind. We chose not to support Internet Explorer early on, and this browser has reached the end of its official support from Microsoft in June 2022. If you are on a Windows device please use the [Microsoft Edge browser](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/microsoft-edge) instead, or one of the common "evergreen" browsers [Chrome, Firefox, and Safari](https://browsehappy.com/).

## How do you add setback lines or curb-to-curb widths?

This is not currently possible in STS Street. We designed STS Street for a lay audience, who may not be fully aware of all the fixed constraints of street design. As a result, we decided not complicate the user interface with setbacks or curb-to-curb dimensions, focusing instead on space between buildings, which is what people experience every day. We plan to add these features in the future for a professional version of STS Street.

## How do you lock down parts of a street to prevent users from editing them?

We believe that civic engagement is most empowering when users are not artificially constrained from expressing their creativity or their opinions. Instead, we believe users should be educated about constraints and be given the right to accept or reject them. Furthermore, a digital sandbox is the perfect environment to experiment with concepts that might be impossible or unrealistic in real life.
